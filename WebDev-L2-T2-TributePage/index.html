THE BEGINNING

From a small island town to the heart of a nation.

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. Coming from a modest family, he grew up with a deep curiosity for learning and a determination to create a meaningful future.

His early fascination with flight and science eventually led him towards aerospace engineering. What began as a childhood dream would become a remarkable scientific journey that contributed to some of India's most important technological programmes.

Kalam's story was never simply about achievement. It was about persistence — continuing forward despite limitations and believing that knowledge could transform both individuals and nations.

02 · THE SCIENTIST

Building the future of India.

Kalam worked with India's Defence Research and Development Organisation and later joined the Indian Space Research Organisation. His work became closely associated with India's growing capabilities in space technology and defence.

He played a significant role in the development of India's indigenous Satellite Launch Vehicle, SLV-III. Its successful launch of the Rohini satellite in 1980 became an important milestone for India's space programme.

05 · THE PRESIDENT

He carried science into the heart of the nation.

In 2002, Kalam became the 11th President of India. Unlike the traditional image of a distant constitutional figure, he became deeply connected with students, teachers and ordinary citizens.

He travelled across the country, spoke at educational institutions and continuously encouraged young people to question, innovate and believe in their ability to shape India's future.

06 · THE LEGACY

REMEMBERED. INSPIRED. CONTINUING.

His greatest achievement was inspiring others.

Kalam's legacy extends far beyond laboratories, awards and public office. He became a symbol of possibility — proof that a person from humble beginnings could dream on a national scale.

His words continue to resonate with students because he never asked them simply to succeed. He asked them to imagine, to learn and to use their knowledge to make a difference.
